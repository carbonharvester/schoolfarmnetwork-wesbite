Commercial farms. A lasting contribution to school meals.

The opportunity

A school’s land can do more for its children.

A reliable school meal needs a reliable source of funding. At schools with suitable underused land, a productive farm can help create one.

School Farm Network brings together the infrastructure, agricultural expertise and routes to market to make that land productive. Our aim is to lower the financial burden on parents or schools while creating paid work in the surrounding community.

How the model works

Grow for a market.
Earn for a purpose.

01

Build the farm

Agree land use with the school and community. Assess water, soil, access and demand. Invest in irrigation, secure boundaries and the infrastructure each site needs.

02

Grow and sell

Paid local teams grow crops selected for the site and its buyers. SFN manages production, quality and sales, with opportunities for young people and women.

03

Contribute and reinvest

Farm income supports operating costs and an agreed contribution to school meals. Reinvestment keeps farms productive and, as the business proves itself, supports expansion.

The crops we sell do not have to be the food children eat. Selling higher-value produce can generate income to help pay for balanced school meals.

Our beginnings

A first harvest.
A foundation to build on.

Our original pilot at Oloosirkon Primary School in Kenya grew approximately four tonnes of onions in its first harvest in May 2026.

It also showed us what a lasting business needs beyond a successful crop.

What we learned

Secure buyers before harvest. Plan for water and drainage. Protect the crop. Build trust with the school and community from the start.

We are developing the next phase around these lessons, including higher-value herbs and stronger buyer relationships. Commercial viability and the contribution to meals must be demonstrated as the model develops.
