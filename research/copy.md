# SFN copy research

Method step 7. Competitor copy pulled live 2026-08-28 from the four most successfully
funded organisations adjacent to SFN's space. Principles derived below, then the banned
list, then the copy deck for the homepage build.

## What the winners actually say

### One Acre Fund (oneacrefund.org) — the flagship in African agriculture
- Headline: "Farmers are the key to achieving food security and prosperity."
- Sub: "In Sub-Saharan Africa, most people living on $1 a day are farmers. One Acre Fund
  supplies farmers with the farm supplies and training they need to grow more food and
  earn more money."
- Sections: "We serve / We work / We supply / We deliver" — parallel active verbs.
- Proof: "45% more farm income on the same plot of land" · "5.9 million farming
  households" · "approximately $600 million in impact" · "10 countries".
- CTA: "Invest in small-scale farms in Sub-Saharan Africa."

**Lesson:** headline is a plain thesis claim, not a slogan. Proof is a specific number
with a comparator ("more income on the same plot"). The funder verb is *invest*, not
*donate*.

### Food for Education (food4education.org) — the partner
- "Hungry kids can't learn." — the best single line in the whole set. Four words,
  concrete, undeniable.
- "The problem is big. We can't afford to think small."
- Weakness: "Explore our why" is a mystery-meat CTA — a label you must decode.

**Lesson:** short declarative sentences with no abstraction beat any amount of copy.
And CTA labels must say where they go.

### charity: water (charitywater.org) — the direct-response benchmark
- Headline: "Bring clean and safe water to every person on the planet" — a verb plus
  totality.
- Proof: exact counters, not rounded ("209,241 water projects", "21,641,908 people").
- "Know that your donation is making a difference" — the proof-promise, stated as a
  reader benefit.

**Lesson:** exact numbers read as audited; round numbers read as marketing. Totality
("every person") is what makes ambition memorable — which validates Matthew's
"every generation".

### Babban Gona (babbangona.com) — the counter-example
- Frames everything through fear: migration crisis, violence, "a downward spiral,"
  "casting a shadow of fear, anxiety, and desperation."
- But: "double profitability vs average farmer" is a strong comparative proof, and
  "Invest in the movement" confirms the invest-verb pattern.

**Lesson (negative):** fear-framing and "WOMEN EMPOWERMENT" section headers are exactly
the register SFN must never use. Positive opportunity frame only.

## Principles applied to SFN

1. **Thesis headline.** A plain claim the reader can disagree with, not a mood.
2. **One idea per sentence.** Aim for Food-for-Education length: 4 to 9 words for the
   lines that matter.
3. **Proof beside claim.** Every number carries its source and its comparator, and sits
   next to the sentence it proves. Exact beats round (approximately 4 tonnes stays
   "approximately" because that is what the records say — honesty is the brand).
4. **Obvious labels.** Nav and CTAs say where they go: "How it works", "The record",
   "Partner with us". Never "Explore our why". (Don't Make Me Think.)
5. **Front-load.** First three words of every heading carry the meaning, because
   scanners read only those.
6. **The funder verb is invest.** One Acre Fund and Babban Gona both ask funders to
   invest, not give. SFN is an operation, so this fits.
7. **You-language at the decision points.** "You contribute the land." "You fund the
   build." (Never "your land" to schools — see fact rule below.)
8. **Positive frame.** Land as opportunity, never children as victims. The one
   emotional line we keep is in Food for Education's register ("Hungry kids can't
   learn"), used once, not milked.
9. **One primary action per view.** Everything else is a text link.

## Banned list

NGO clichés (all found on the competitor sites): empower, empowerment, transforming
lives, unlock the potential, changemakers, beneficiaries, giving back, uplift,
vulnerable communities, join the movement, sustainable future, at the heart of.

AI tells: journey, delve, harness, leverage, holistic, seamless, innovative, game
changing, making an impact, testament, beacon, vibrant, nestled, "more than just".

Mechanics (Matthew's standing rules): no em dashes; never headline the tonnage; "every
generation", never "a generation"; the Food for Education payment is future tense until
it has happened; nothing invented, ever.

## Copy deck for the homepage

**FACT RULE (Matthew, 2026-08-28): schools do not own the land.** No copy may assert
school ownership. The sanctioned verb from the original brief is *contribute*: schools
contribute land and receive an agreed benefit, and carry no operating risk. Who holds
title varies and is one of the assessment checks; add "land tenure arrangement per
school" to the verification list. Say the land is *there*, *at* schools, *unused* —
never *theirs* or *owned*.

**Headline (FINAL, Matthew's own, 2026-08-28):**
> The land is already there. The system isn't.

Two-beat thesis carrying the whole pitch: the missing piece is not land, money or
demand, it is the operation. Sets up "we built the system" as SFN's answer everywhere
downstream. Do not change without Matthew.

**Matthew's own rationale (2026-08-28), verbatim — this is the voice brief for every
line on the site, and judge criteria for all copy:**
- It creates tension.
- It immediately suggests the opportunity is existing infrastructure being underused.
- "The system isn't" points toward what SFN actually builds: operators, infrastructure,
  agronomy, offtake, data, training, financing.
- It differentiates you from a school-garden charity without saying "we're not a school
  garden."
- It leaves room for SFN to become much bigger than simply a farm operator.
- Visually, it has the confidence of a serious infrastructure company rather than an
  NGO homepage.

**Derived copy tests (apply to every section):** Does the line create tension or
resolve one? Does it read as infrastructure, not charity? Does it name real components
of the system (operators, agronomy, offtake, data, training, financing) rather than
abstractions? Would a school-garden charity be unable to say it?

**Subheadline (FINAL, Matthew's own, 2026-08-28):**
> School Farm Network turns underused land at African schools into professionally
> operated farms that produce food, create jobs, train the next generation of farmers
> and help fund school meals.

Note the hedge "help fund" on meals: keep it, it is the honest verb until the Food for
Education payment has happened.

**Primary CTA:** Partner with us · **Secondary:** See how it works

**Section spine (scannable, front-loaded):**
1. **The problem, in one line:** "Hungry students sit beside empty fields." (our one
   emotional line, F4E register, land-framed not victim-framed)
2. **How it works:** "We assess. We build. We farm. We account." (One Acre Fund
   parallel-verb pattern; each expands to one sentence)
3. **The record:** "First harvest: approximately 4 tonnes of onions, weighed at
   Seeds2Education, Kenya, May 2026." Source line beneath. Then, plainly: what is under
   way (sale to market, proceeds to Food for Education, future tense), and what is not
   yet measured (cost per meal), stated as absent.
4. **The ambition (close):** "Feed every generation." — totality line, charity: water
   pattern, Matthew's own words.
5. **Audience split (Arva pattern):** "I run a school" / "I fund infrastructure" /
   "I buy produce" — each one line plus one verb-CTA.

**Stat band (One Acre Fund shape, our verified numbers only):**
- "~4 tonnes — first harvest, weighed, May 2026"
- "2 schools — in the current pilot"
- "1 system — every farm runs on the same record"
- (No fourth invented number. Three is what we can prove.)
