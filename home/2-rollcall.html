Page 01

A school can hold land for years without anyone marking it.

A school day has periods with nothing scheduled on them. A school site has ground with nothing planted on it. Nobody keeps a record of either, so nothing changes.

Entering a school in the register is not paperwork. It is the point at which somebody walks the ground, measures what is free, checks the water and marks the boundary. Until that happens there is nothing to plan, nothing to cost and nothing to argue for.

Page 04

The lesson is the work, not a poster about the work.

Each row ties a real action on the plot to what a student does with it. Curriculum links are drafted and need checking against the syllabus.

Measure the plot Area, spacing and quantity, worked out on the ground rather than on a worksheet. Verify: subject and year group
Test soil and water What the soil is short of, what the crop needs, and what the water will carry. Verify: subject and year group
Plan a cycle Working backwards from a harvest date to a planting date. Verify: subject and year group
Keep the books What the cycle cost, what it earned, and whether it was worth running. Verify: subject and year group
Do the paid work Real operational experience, with roles prioritised for women and for people aged 18 to 35. A route into agriculture as a job, not a lesson about one.
